10 P.M., Nov 24th, at a coffee shop in Dist 7, HCMC.

Me, a fresh graduate one year out of university, surrounded by the enthusiasm and the energy of the youngsters around me: university students working together to finish their projects. I hear the laughter, the enjoyment, the discussion, and a faint sense of stress. And, to be honest with myself, I miss those times a lot: gathering with my friends to finish the last-semester project, or just discussing technical news, personal workflows with each other.

We rarely meet each other now, maybe twice or thrice a year. We still talk, through Messenger, sharing technical blogs, opinions, things we have built, but it's not the same energy we had during our student years.

Life has been all hustle and bustle lately. Every day, a new technology is released. If I don't catch up, I will fall behind. This makes me crave information daily: scrolling through Hacker News, reading every tech post, asking ChatGPT to run a workflow of gathering news... I do all of it, telling myself it's good for me.

And, it finally comes. The day that I burn out. I cannot take in any more knowledge. My forehead burns, and my brain refuses to process what I read. I try different things, resting myself for 20-30 minutes to ease my head, staying away from the screen, taking a stroll. Nothing works. It feels like I hit a wall, a physical limit of my brain, like a room filled with water, about to overflow.

Why? Is this the price of being a developer? Is this because of AI? Just why?

Or maybe it's just me being childish. Just the complaints of someone who has barely stepped into life, fresh out of school, and can't handle the flood.

Anyway, I realized that I can't hold everything in my head, so I built a system to offload some of it. I read _Building a Second Brain_ by Tiago Forte, _Getting Things Done_ (well, the first chapter; its writing style isn't my favorite), adopted the LLM Wiki patterns, and finally built Janus, an agent that helps me run my life.

Hmmm, it feels like I'm still missing something. I can offload knowledge to my agent, but offloading isn't learning. The knowledge lives in my agent, not in me. So here we are again: yet another "write a blog to distill your knowledge and share it with others". I don't dislike the idea, but it makes me feel like a robot, just another typical person, not standing out from anyone.

I know most of us want to stand out, to be a unique version of ourselves in this crazy world. But following a common pattern isn't a bad thing at all. Only by following a pattern can you change it to your own version. That's what makes you unique. Janus started the same way: someone else's books, someone else's patterns, until it became mine.

Maybe I'm still being childish. But you don't know until you try. So this is me trying: no more collecting, just writing what I actually understand. Kind of like those late nights with my friends, except this time I'm finishing the project alone, and you're reading it.
